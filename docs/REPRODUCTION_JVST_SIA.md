# Reproducing the JVST A and SIA papers with this package

This page says, figure by figure, what a reader can recompute from public
material, what needs material requested from the authors, and what cannot be
regenerated by anyone. It also records what has actually been run. It is
written for the two papers that use the self-supervised DNN denoiser and this
package's depth inversion:

- **JVST A** — denoising strategies for 3-D visualisation of AR-HAXPES depth
  profiles: [10.1116/6.0005585](https://doi.org/10.1116/6.0005585)
- **SIA** — cross-exposure transferability of self-supervised DNN denoising:
  [10.1002/sia.70123](https://doi.org/10.1002/sia.70123)
- **The deposit** — concept DOI
  [10.5281/zenodo.22092108](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22092108); the
  version this page is pinned to is **v1.0.0,
  [10.5281/zenodo.22092109](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22092109)**. The
  concept DOI resolves to the latest version, so a manifest must cite the
  version DOI and the file hashes, never the concept DOI alone.

## Three different things called "reproduction"

| Kind | What a pass establishes | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Stored-result check** | From the deposited depth profiles `D_map` and pseudo-ground-truth `D_ref`, the published metrics, tables and figure annotations are recomputed with the published definitions | that those profiles follow correctly from the raw frames |
| **Fixed-weight re-run** | With the same inputs, preprocessing, trained weights and physics settings, inference and the depth inversion agree with the deposited profiles within a stated tolerance | that retraining would reach the same conclusion |
| **Retraining** | With the stated split, training conditions and several seeds, the defined statistics and conclusions recur | generalisation to another instrument, sample or noise regime |

A second axis is the **input set**: *public deposit only*, or *deposit plus
material provided by the authors on request*. A result reached with provided
material is never reported as "reproducible from public artifacts".

**Status (2026-10-18):** the stored-result check has been run and is recorded
below. The fixed-weight re-run and retraining have **not** been started; what
they need is listed in the last sections.

**Who has run it.** The implementer, on the development machine and in an
isolated virtual environment there; an independent auditor (a separate
session on the same machine, re-extracting the printed values from the paper);
and a clean-venv verification session (also on the same machine, Python 3.11
and 3.12 environments) that supplied the SciPy 1.18.1 re-run quoted above.
A reproduction by a third party, on another machine (Linux included) or by
another person, is planned for after publication and is **not** a condition
for the first release; until it has happened this page does not claim one.

## The stored-result check: `deppro.haxpes.deposit_replay`

```bash
# extract sia_figure_data.zip and jvst_figure_data.zip from the v1.0.0 record, then
python -m deppro.haxpes.deposit_replay \
    --sia-dir  path/to/sia_figure_data \
    --jvst-h5  path/to/jvst_figure_data/figure_data.h5 \
    --out replay.json
```

The module pins every file it reads by the SHA-256 the deposit publishes in
`SHA256SUMS.txt`; a missing or altered file raises and nothing is compared.
It needs `deppro`, NumPy, SciPy and h5py; it does not read any
`DEPPRO_*` environment variable, any author path or any cache.

Its metric definitions are transcribed from the verbatim figure scripts:

- **depth RMSE (%)** — mean over pixels of the per-pixel RMSE over
  (depth, element), ×100 (paper Eq. 2; `fig4_hax26_equiv_exposure.py`). This
  is *not* the pooled RMSE that `arhaxpes_denoise.metrics.depth_rmse` returns
  when given a whole map; the two differ by Jensen's inequality (e.g.
  4.385 % vs 4.784 % for the 4.8 s / 24 s-trained cell).
- **W1 (Å)** — per element, the median over pixels of
  W1(`D_ref`, `D_map[px]`) on the 30–600 Å grid; then the mean over elements.
- **K_eq** — the frame-average ladder at K ∈ {1, 5, 10, 25, 50, 125} is
  fitted with the script's offset-origin power law with a fixed floor
  (RMSE: √(A(K+K₀)^(-2γ) + 0.5²); W1: B(K+K₀)^(-γ) + 0.5), using the
  script's starting values, bounds and `maxfev`; K_eq is the inverse of the
  fitted curve at the denoiser's value, accepted only in [0.5, 500].
- **Seed pilot (Figs. S9, S10)** — per-pixel RMSE (%) mean and standard
  deviation per (target, training source, seed).
- **JVST metrics** — `rrmse` (median over valid finite pixels ×100),
  `sigma_thk` (population std of valid thicknesses), `n_valid`
  (`thk_map > 0`), and `w1` as above but computed over valid pixels, following
  the authors' analysis script. The stored `depth_rmse` field has no
  definition in the deposit; two candidate forms are tried and the result is
  labelled *inferred*.

The published reference values and the match rule are fixed in the module
(`PUBLISHED_SIA`): a K_eq matches when it rounds to the integer in Table 1; a
depth RMSE or W1 matches when it rounds to the one-decimal value quoted in the
text. Nothing was adjusted after seeing results.

**Reproducing the record itself.** The verdicts (23/23, 14/14, 69/69, 0/65) do
not depend on the library versions, and the metric values recomputed from the
arrays (depth RMSE, W1) reproduce to better than relative 1e-9. The *fitted
ladder coefficients* (`A`, `B`, `gamma`, `N0`) and the K_eq derived from them
depend slightly on the optimiser: an independent re-run with SciPy 1.18.1
reproduced `A`, `B`, `gamma` and the K_eq to ≤ 3.1e-8 relative. Four of the
six `N0` values sit on the lower bound 0 (1e-17 to 1e-22) and have no
meaningful relative difference; compare them with an absolute floor instead.
A re-run therefore agrees when `A`, `B`, `gamma` and K_eq match to **relative
1e-6**, `N0` to **absolute 1e-12**, and every verdict is identical; the
environment is recorded in the JSON but is not pinned.

**Which reference.** Both papers define the pseudo-GT *per dataset*: the JVST
text says "the pseudoground truth for each dataset was defined as the average
over all temporal frames" (fix25: all 2 500 frames; fix23: all 400), and SIA
§2.5 defines one pseudo-GT per exposure and evaluates each run against the
pseudo-GT of its own exposure. The replay follows that: SIA uses the three
per-exposure files the Fig. 4 script names; JVST uses
`ground_truth/<dataset>/D_ref` of the HDF5 file (fix25 = the authors'
`session102_pseudo_gt_9ang.npz`, which every JVST figure script except Fig. 1
reads; fix23 = `session102f_pseudo_gt.npz`). No reference is shared across
datasets.

For fix25, the stored `w1` (and `depth_rmse`) values are not reproduced from
the deposited `D_map` against **either** deposited pseudo-GT
(`ground_truth/fix25`, the canonical one, or `ground_truth/fix25_legacy`).
The stored means differ from the recomputation against the canonical GT by
−13 % to +4 % (median +0.8 %, over the 65 compared conditions); against the
legacy GT the difference is far larger. The thickness and fit-residual statistics, which do not involve a
reference profile, match exactly, and the fix23 `w1` computed with the same
function matches exactly, so the function and the arrays are not in question.
The stored fix25 metrics JSON (`session102u_w1_all.json`, 2026-03-15, and
`session103u_w1_all.json`, 2026-06-02, byte-identical to the HDF5 strings)
was produced by scripts that read `session102u_pseudo_gt.npz`; that file was
regenerated on 2026-04-12 and the deposited `fix25_legacy` is the regenerated
version. Among the 13 pseudo-GT files found in the authors' JVST cache and
output directories on 2026-10-18, none reproduces the stored values; the
canonical `_9ang` file is the closest. The reference the stored fix25 metrics
were computed against is therefore not available, in the deposit or locally.
This is a finding about the deposit's internal consistency, reported here and
not resolved. The fix25 metrics *can* be recomputed from the deposited arrays
— they then equal the "recomputed" column of the record, not the stored JSON;
whether the JVST paper's printed fix25 W1 values come from the stored JSON was
not checked in this session.

No JVST figure script is in the deposit, and the JVST paper's printed numbers
were not pinned in this session; the JVST part is therefore an internal
consistency check of the deposit, not a check against the JVST paper.

## Settings that differ between the historical run and the current code

- **Polar offset (formerly called the sample tilt).** All deposited arrays and
  figures were made with 26.0° in `deppro.haxpes.config` (then `tilt_angle` /
  `SAMPLE_TILT_DEG`, now `polar_offset` / `SAMPLE_POLAR_DEG`); since
  2026-09-27 the code default is 30.0° (the published JVST text states 30°).
  A fixed-weight re-run that aims to reproduce the deposited
  profiles must set 26.0° explicitly and record it in its manifest; a
  re-analysis with 30.0° is a different result and gets a different manifest.
  The stored-result check above is unaffected: it only reads deposited
  profiles.
- **Window W.** The SIA Self/Cross-DNN results use W = 1 (`*_dn_W1`); JVST
  explores W = 1…50; the deposit's generic example and `dnndenoiser`'s
  documentation use W = 5 as "canonical". A recipe must state W per run.
- **Compound table / IMFP source.** The depth inversion resolves its
  compound table through `DEPPRO_COMPOUND_TABLE_PATH`, else `toyomacro`; the
  historical runs used the authors' CSV. The table, its version and hash must
  be part of a fixed-weight manifest.
- **Denoiser class path.** `models.network` (pre-publication checkout) →
  `dnndenoiser.models.network` (published). Weights are interchangeable.

## What a fixed-weight re-run would need (not started)

Nothing below is authorised by this page; raw data and weights are used only
with the owner's explicit approval of the exact file set.

1. **Weights and normalisation**: per element (Si 1s, Ti 1s, Al 1s, C 1s,
   O 1s) and per training exposure (4.8 / 24 / 120 s for SIA; fix25 / fix23
   and W for JVST) the `model_<Elem>_fold<n>.pt` and `norm_<Elem>.json`
   files, with their SHA-256 and the run identifier they came from.
2. **Inputs**: the binned frame stacks (`hax26_*_stacks_cache.npz` and the
   fix25 / fix23 equivalents), or the raw `.ibw`/`.pxt` files plus the exact
   binning, angle-mask (5–55°), frame order and energy-axis conventions used.
3. **Physics settings**: polar offset 26.0° (`polar_offset`, formerly
   `tilt_angle`), the compound table and cross-section
   source used at the time, the L1 solver configuration (grid 20 × 30 Å,
   regularisation, initial values, stopping rule) — all readable from
   `deppro.haxpes.config` / `pipeline.py` at the deposited scripts' era and to
   be frozen into a manifest.
4. **Cost**: the denoiser inference is cheap (one ResNet-FCNN pass per
   spectrum). The inversion cost is per pixel and was not measured in this
   session; it should be timed on a handful of pixels of one condition before
   any 2 500- or 10 000-pixel grid is attempted.
5. **Acceptance**: per-pixel agreement of `D_map`, `thk_map`, `rrmse_map`
   with the deposited arrays at a tolerance fixed *before* the run, then the
   metrics via `deposit_replay`'s functions.

## What retraining would need (not started)

The public `dnndenoiser` CLI trains the moving-average method from a frame
stack (`train --method moving-average --window W`) and, at the current `main`,
also runs `infer` on a frame stack; the frame-stack schema, `frame_index`
semantics and the normalisation contract are documented there. A retraining
study additionally needs the training subset per exposure, the seeds, the
train / evaluation split and its leakage unit, and the statistic and tolerance
to be compared — all fixed before training. Training cost scales with
12 500 / 2 500 / 500 frames × 5 elements × seeds and was not estimated here.
